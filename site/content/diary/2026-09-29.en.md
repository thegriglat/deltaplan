({{< commit 0a3114d >}}). ## False hang on exit

The build agent saw 5/10 hangs of the built game on exit and suspected the cloud shader cache → the cache was removed ({{< commit b26234d >}}). gdb on the hung process: the main thread waits on join, worker threads sleep. On a clean main — 0/25 (a free machine, 100 % CPU, a second copy of the game). Conclusion: an artifact of the agent's builds from a working copy with unfinished edits. Lesson: check on a clean tree before "fixing".

## Slope flow — from formulas to physics

The consulting pilot: "the flow is not physical". Research ([docs/research/slope_wind.md](/docs/research/slope_wind.md)): we have no wind acceleration at the brow, the lift band is a fixed 250 m, the "pipe" in a saddle is the opposite. The consulting pilot's answer — numerical reference points: acceleration up to ~2 heights of the mountain from the foot, laminar at 2.5 H; steeper — stronger; a saddle ×1.5 along the axis even with a 15° oblique wind. The course of the discussion:
- formulas (option A) → a mass-consistent wind field (the Poisson equation) on the GPU → linearity in wind (2 base fields per stability level) → nested clipmap grids 50…800 m around the pilot (the user's idea: "tree depth by distance") → plan [docs/archive/plan/wind-field.md](/docs/archive/plan/wind-field.md) (~31 agent-days);
- CUDA — no (pilots have AMD), Rust + rayon — considered, Vulkan compute chosen;
- a **coupled system** "heating → buoyancy → mass flow" (the user's idea: a cellular automaton — a hot cell gives heat sideways and up, draws in mass from the cold ones; then "layer→layer transition by multiplying by a matrix of transport coefficients", "6 runs along the axes", "a kernel as in image processing"). The physical basis: 5 laws (mass, momentum with Archimedes and nonlocal pressure, energy, water, equation of state), essentially the Boussinesq approximation.
- A prototype on a 2D ridge cross-section ([tools/research/heat_ca/](/tools/research/heat_ca/), CuPy on an RTX 4070 SUPER, {{< commit 50f1680 >}}): over a heated slope — lift up to ~1.5 m/s, inflow at the foot, spreading over ~1.4 km, sinking around; convergence by cell — from 50 m it changes by 5–10 %; the explicit scheme in 3D is too slow (50 m — ~17 min) → experiments 1–6 (linearity, Picard+CG, transport matrices, ADI, kernels) are with the prototype coordinator.

## Principle: physical correctness within the model

The user: "What the consulting pilot likes is a reference point, not a requirement… any simulation is a model, any model has boundaries"; "under 'likes' you can tweak coefficients endlessly — a dead end". Hence the task WF-16: calibration by the Professor scheme (HEP): runs → polynomials → χ² against numerical reference points with uncertainties. A search for tabular data (literature of the 70s–90s and measurements) was launched.

## Results of the prototype experiments

({{< commit 54753a2 >}}, {{< commit 960ebec >}}, {{< commit 457928d >}}, {{< commit 75f5698 >}}, summary — {{< commit 60d11fd >}}, [tools/research/heat_ca/out/summary.md](/tools/research/heat_ca/out/summary.md), section "Layers as matrices"). Criterion — physics first, then accuracy, then GPU cost:
- 1, 3 — superposition and linear kernels: linear only when lift is centimetres per second; at 250 W/m² the superposition error is 69 %, a thermal from a sum of kernels is three times weaker. The user: "not impressive".
- 6 — the "jet kernel" (a chain of formulas: heating blur, warm pool, buoyant jet, Prandtl, wave, projection): 1–2 ms, thermal place/strength/ceiling ±5–30 %, the field is coarse. Good as an instant estimate.
- 4 — layer→layer transition matrices (the user's idea: harmonics in x, a 4×4 block sweep up/down, repeats with Anderson): the same fixed point, 1–2 %, 56–66 repeats almost independent of the cell (200…12.5 m), 0.33 s versus 24.6 s by steps at 12.5 m.
- 5 — ADI sweeps: the same point, 100–190 rounds, but an iteration is still expensive.
- **2 — Picard** (Oseen momentum + projection with one V-cycle + heat): ≤ 0.5 % in wind, ~1 % in θ′, ×5…×36 faster than steps; a 3D estimate over a 200 m area — 2–5 s. Chosen for the game.
- Finding: the baseline references are under-converged (an absolute settling criterion) — by 3–8 % in wind and 10–23 % in θ′ from the exact fixed point; all executors found it independently.
- Convergence by cell: 200 m — no slope wind, 100 m — the coarsest with the right picture, 50 m — lift with an error of ~15 %, finer than 25 m makes no sense.

## Three scales of the air model

The user: the feeling of flight is "general truthfulness (a thermal over the field)" and "local disturbances (the wing is shaken, as in reality)". Hence: (1) the mean field — Picard on the GPU, 200 m over the area and 100/50 m windows around the pilot, once per game hour; (2) thermals — the current bubbles, but place, strength (Deardorff's w*), ceiling and drift come from the field; (3) turbulence and jolts — the current form, strength from the field's shear and heating. Scales 2 and 3 have no knobs of their own for "where and how much". The main question at the junction is double counting of heat: the field's convective vertical goes to the pilot only through bubbles with the same mass flux. Thermals in the network — a list of sources from the host (or quantization). The plan [docs/plan/air_model.md](/docs/plan/air_model.md) ({{< commit 0f07933 >}}, ~31 days, gate after a 3D reference on CuPy) replaced the mass-consistent wind-field plan: that one had neither heat nor inertia.
