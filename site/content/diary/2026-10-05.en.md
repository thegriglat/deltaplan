## The new wind network
The solver finished the fields for 305 hang gliding sites (285 for training and 20 held out from other regions) and the 4 game sites — 24 conditions per site, 7416 cases. 11.9 % of cases did not converge, none diverged.

A network of the same P2 architecture trained for 132 epochs (1.9 h). At the author's request, weight snapshots were kept every 5 epochs and the validation error was logged every epoch, to catch overfitting. The validation error stopped falling after about epoch 100, the held-out error after about epoch 80; the last two dozen epochs gained less than 1 %. The gap between train 0.155 and validation 0.26 shows that the limit is the number of sites, not epochs.

Wind error at 60 m (median / 90 % of points / relative), first training run ({{< commit 58ca50f >}}):

| | previous network | new |
|---|---|---|
| 20 held-out sites | 0.92 / 2.90 / 23 % | 0.69 / 2.25 / 17 % |
| 4 game sites | 0.76 / 2.60 / 19 % | 0.59 / 1.93 / 15 % |

An inflow profile without terrain gives 2.24 m/s. The previous network had seen three of the game sites in training, so the comparison there favours it; it had not seen the held-out sites. The game got the network retrained on all sites ({{< commit 4b7da5e >}}), with its training range written into the file — winds up to 12 m/s. Review caught that without this the game would have clamped the wind to 8 m/s.

The author considered measuring the error-versus-number-of-sites curve but decided not to invest in it: earlier runs gave −7 % per doubling of sites, and in light wind and convection the reference itself is unstable. The network direction is closed.

## A wind phase diagram
The author's idea: look at the wind computation the way a physical chemist would. Right now one solver (a steady field via Picard iteration) and one network try to describe every situation. If the solution classes are separated — where there is a single steady field, where there are several, where only statistics exist — the general solver breaks up into specialised ones for each "state of matter" of the air. The analogue is flow-regime maps for two-phase flow in pipes.

Axes: the Froude number (wind versus stability over terrain), signed heating (−z_i/L), slope steepness, and terrain height relative to the mixed layer. The behaviour of the Picard iteration became a probe of the phases. Over the 7320 existing cases:
- non-convergence is driven mainly by light wind: below 1 m/s — 79 %, 1.5–2 m/s — 47 %, from 3 m/s — 2–3 %; at a fixed wind it falls as Fr grows;
- the earlier guess that thermals cause non-convergence did not hold: without heating slightly more cases fail;
- near the boundary converged cases need more iterations — critical slowing down;
- flow separation behind ridges appears from slopes of 20–25°.

![Solver non-convergence versus the Froude number](/docs/research/air_phase/fig_nonconv_fr_hg.png)

The author suggested that some phases can be described almost analytically, and that the field could be assembled in GPU shaders without a network: each cell uses the mechanism of its phase. Document: [docs/research/air_phase.md](/docs/research/air_phase.md), second part — [analytics by phase and field assembly](/docs/research/air_phase_experts.md) ({{< commit 93e267d >}}). What is physics and what is a property of our solver, and where the boundaries are sharp, still has to be tested with runs on idealised terrain.
